Path to licensure in Texas
  1. Meet the prerequisites. Be 18+, a U.S. citizen or lawfully admitted alien, and meet TREC's honesty/trustworthiness standard.
  2. Complete TREC-approved education. The Professional modules above total 154 classroom hours plus the 40-hour practicum. Hours for the Apprentice and Real Estate Inspector levels are listed on TREC's website.
  3. Complete the 40-hour practicum. Field training with a licensed inspector; write six reports on the REI 7-6 form, five of which must meet standard criteria. †
  4. Apply to TREC. Applications go through TREC's REALM portal, and you have one year from filing to meet every requirement. See TREC's current fee schedule for the application fee.
  5. Pass both exams. The NHIE and the Texas Real Estate Inspector Law exam, both via Pearson VUE. Each section is passed separately and stays valid for one year. Failing three times requires additional education.
  6. Submit proof of insurance. Liability coverage of at least $100,000 per occurrence / $100,000 aggregate.
  7. Submit fingerprints. Required by the Texas Department of Public Safety for a background check.
Continuing education

Texas inspectors complete 32 hours of continuing education every two-year renewal cycle, including an 8-hour Legal & Ethics / Standards of Practice review. No more than 16 hours may come from a single topic.

TREC (Texas Real Estate Commission) is the licensing authority; a home inspector license must be renewed every two years from the date of issuance.

SOP scope — what's excluded (§535.227)

A Texas real estate inspection is a limited visual survey and basic performance evaluation — not a comprehensive or exploratory investigation. Inspectors are not required to: use specialized equipment (thermal imaging, moisture meters, drones); inspect anything buried, hidden, or concealed; inspect elevators, detached structures, or concrete flatwork; determine the presence of pests, mold, asbestos, or lead; determine cause, code compliance, life expectancy, or insurability; or give cost estimates. An inspector may depart from a required inspection item if the client agrees, the inspector isn't qualified, or a hazard prevents it — but must disclose the departure and the reason in the report.

Prohibited acts & discipline (Occ. Code Ch. 1102, Subch. G)

An inspector may not accept an assignment where the fee or employment is contingent on reporting a predetermined condition, and may not act dishonestly, fraudulently, or with deceit. Course materials separate three violations (Occ. Code §§1102.301–1102.302): dishonesty is intentionally omitting or softening a finding (often for money or referrals); negligence is carelessly failing to report something the inspector should have caught; incompetence is not knowing a requirement existed at all. Acting as both the inspector and a broker/agent on the same transaction is illegal as a conflicting capacity, regardless of dishonesty.

Texas abolished the real estate inspection recovery fund effective September 1, 2013. Inspectors now carry their own financial responsibility: liability insurance of at least $100,000 per occurrence and $100,000 aggregate, or a bond or other security TREC accepts (Occ. Code §1102.1141). Two other prohibitions: an inspector may not perform or agree to perform repairs in connection with an inspection under an earnest money contract, lease, or exchange (§1102.304), and may not act as an undisclosed principal or as a broker or salesperson in the same transaction (§1102.303).

Report form: REI 7-6 †

TREC requires its own promulgated report form. Each system/component is marked I (Inspected), NI (Not Inspected), NP (Not Present), or D (Deficient) — and every D requires a written comment describing the specific deficiency observed, since a checkbox alone isn't sufficient documentation.
